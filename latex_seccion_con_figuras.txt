The F1-Score is utilized as the primary evaluation metric to account for the intrinsic class imbalances within the medical datasets.

Within the pulmonary domain, the Pneumonia dataset proved to be the least challenging (F1-Scores near 0.94), while COVID-19 presented the greatest difficulty. The results indicate that an increase in the number of thresholds from $D=3$ to $D=12$ leads to a consistent degradation of classification performance across all algorithms. This suggests that over-segmentation fractures the diffuse density gradients of lung tissues, introducing noise into the GLCM radiomic features.

In this experimental block, Brain Tumors yielded the highest F1-Scores (exceeding 0.95), while Stroke lesions proved more complex to classify. The empirical data shows that $D=12$ configurations dominate the top performance tiers. This validates the premise that accurately mapping the structural heterogeneity of intracranial tissues necessitates high-dimensional thresholding to preserve radiomic integrity.
